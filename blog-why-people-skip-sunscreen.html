Why People Skip Sunscreen

By Jenna Sim

In our last post, we looked at what people believe about sunscreen. This time we’re looking at what people actually do with it!

We asked how often people apply sunscreen when they’re spending time outdoors. 26.4% said always and 31.9% said often. After that, 24.2% said sometimes, 9.9% said rarely, and 7.7% said never. When you add up the bottom three, about four in ten people skip sunscreen at least some of the time. That’s a lot, especially since 47.3% of people rated daily sun protection a 5 out of 5. Almost half of the people we talked to think it’s extremely important, but fewer than three in ten always do it.

Our survey didn’t ask people why they skip sunscreen, but there are some reasons that come up a lot whenever people talk about this. The main ones include forgetting, not liking how it feels, the cost, worrying about acne, and not having time. We want to go through each one and give you something that might help.

If you forget, the easiest fix is to put sunscreen where you’ll see it. Keep a bottle in your bag, next to your keys, or on your bathroom counter next to your toothbrush. If you’d rather not rely on remembering, set an alarm on your phone for right before you usually leave the house.

If it feels greasy or heavy, a lot of that comes down to which type you pick. Sunscreen comes in lotions, gels, sprays, sticks, and lightweight fluids, and they don’t all feel the same. If the thick kind bothers you, try a gel or a product labeled lightweight or non-greasy. It’s worth trying a few, because the one you like wearing is the one you’ll use.

If you’re worried about acne, look for sunscreens labeled non-comedogenic, which means they’re made not to clog pores. Some people with acne-prone skin find that mineral sunscreens with zinc oxide are gentler, though everyone’s skin is different. If you’re not sure, a dermatologist can help you choose!

If cost is a problem, you don’t need an expensive brand. Drugstore sunscreens work just as well as long as the label says broad-spectrum and SPF 30 or higher.

If you don’t have time, try attaching it to something you already do. Put it on right after you wash your face or brush your teeth in the morning. It takes less than a minute, and after a while you won’t have to think about it.

One more result worth mentioning is that 12.1% of people said they aren’t sure which SPF they use. In that case, check the bottle. Broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher is what dermatologists recommend, and 42.9% of our respondents said they usually use SPF 50 or higher, so a lot of people are already at or above that. And if you’re in the 9.9% who said you don’t use sunscreen at all, starting with a broad-spectrum SPF 30 is a good first step.

We hope this was a helpful guide for figuring out where to start. Thank you for reading this week’s blog and we will see you next week!
