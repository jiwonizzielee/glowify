How Well Do People Actually Apply Sunscreen?

By Hanna Zhang

Many people probably don’t realize that wearing sunscreen and wearing it correctly are two different things.

From our survey, 42.9% of people said they usually use SPF 50 or higher, which is the highest category we asked about. Another 30.8% said they use SPF 30. At the same time, 75.8% of people have been sunburned at least once, 38.5% have been burned a few times, and 11% many times. A high SPF doesn’t help much if you use too little or never reapply it. So here’s what dermatologists recommend for putting sunscreen on correctly.

First, use enough. SPF numbers are tested using a thick layer of sunscreen, which is more than most people put on. A thin layer gives you less protection than the number on the bottle says. The two-finger rule is an easy way to measure. Squeeze a line of sunscreen along the length of your index and middle fingers, and that’s about the right amount for your face and neck. For your whole body, the usual recommendation is about one ounce, which is roughly a shot glass.

Second, put it on before you go out. Sunscreen works best when you apply it about 15 minutes before you’re in the sun, so it has time to settle onto your skin. If you put it on while you’re already outside, you’re staying unprotected for the first few minutes.

Third, reapply every two hours. Sunscreen wears off over time, and it comes off faster when you sweat or swim. Even if a bottle says water-resistant, that only lasts for a set amount of time, usually 40 or 80 minutes, and then you need to put more on. Reapply right after you get out of the water or towel off. If you’ve been outside all afternoon and only applied once in the morning, most of the protection is usually gone.

Next, don't skip the spots people usually miss. These include your ears, the back of your neck, the tops of your feet and hands, and your lips, where you can use a lip balm with SPF. If you have thinning hair or a part, your scalp can burn too. These areas get just as much sun as your face does, but they’re easy to forget because you can’t see them as easily. After you put on sunscreen, do a quick check of these spots. It only takes a few seconds!

Finally, check your bottle. Sunscreen expires, usually after about three years. If you can’t remember when you bought it, it’s probably good to buy a new sunscreen. Keeping these will significantly improve your skin protection in the daily.

Sources: American Academy of Dermatology (AAD), UCLA Health
